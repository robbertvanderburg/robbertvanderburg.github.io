01 / Current research

Modeling uncertainty in complex networks.

How likely is it that several parts of a power grid fail during the same storm? Wind, floods and heatwaves affect whole regions, exposing nearby components to similar stresses.

I develop probabilistic models that account for this spatial dependence. I compare Ising models, Bayesian hierarchical models, autologistic regression and independent failure models to study how these assumptions change estimates of network reliability.

This work is part of the Vidi project Power Network Optimization in the Age of Climate Extremes. My supervisors are Alessandro Zocca and Frank van der Meulen.

02 / Research areas

Random walks, reliability & opinion dynamics

  1. Random walks & Markov chains

    With Thao Le, Bernd Heidergott, Ines Lindner and Alessandro Zocca, I study how edge weights change hitting times, Kemeny constants and network optimization.

    Read the traversal costs preprint

  2. Critical infrastructure reliability

    I study how to distinguish dependence that comes from the network itself from correlations driven by shared weather conditions.

    NetSci presentation (PDF)

  3. Opinion dynamics & stochastic systems

    I explore connections between quasi-stationary distributions and models of opinion formation, including the DeGroot and Friedkin–Johnsen models. This grew out of my undergraduate work on metastability.
